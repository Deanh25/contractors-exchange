What actually changes

Six differences from the messenger you have today

Each one is small on its own; together they are the whole gap between a message log and a conversation.

Deal events

Events become chips, not your bubbles

“Accepted the request” and “Deal marked completed” are system events, so they sit centered and quiet.

Today they are stored as messages from whoever clicked, so they render as your own orange bubbles.
Layout

One split view, not two pages

Threads stay on the left while you read and reply, so switching conversations never reloads the screen.

Today the inbox and a conversation are separate pages. Phones keep that flow.
Rhythm

Grouped by sender and by day

Back-to-back messages stack under one avatar with a single timestamp, under a day divider.

Today every message repeats its own timestamp, so a fast exchange reads as clutter.
The deal

The order rides along at the top

The pinned panel carries the same milestone rail as the order page, so the next step is always in view.

Today the panel shows the deal but not where it sits in the process.
Presence

Seen receipts and typing

A quiet “Seen” under your last message, and dots when the other side is writing.

The database already records when each side last opened a thread, so receipts need no new fields.
Content

Files, reactions, and replies

Send a spec PDF, react to a message, and quote the line you are answering.

Today a message carries one image and nothing else.
How it ships

Three rounds, each tested and signed off before the next

Phase 1 is the whole visual change above. Phases 2 and 3 make it live and let it carry real work.

Round one

Layout and feel

  • Split view, with the phone flow unchanged
  • Grouped bubbles and day dividers
  • Deal events as event chips
  • Seen receipts from existing data
  • Enter sends, auto-scroll, sticky composer
No schema change
Round two

It goes live

  • New messages arrive without a refresh
  • Your own message appears the instant you send
  • Typing indicator
  • New-message email, honoring notification preferences
Adds a polling endpoint
Round three

Richer messages

  • Several photos or videos in one message
  • PDF and document attachments
  • Emoji reactions on a message
  • Reply quoting the message above
Adds four small tables/columns

What I need from you

The split view is the one structural decision here. Everything else layers on top of it, and the phone experience keeps today’s inbox-then-conversation flow either way.

The event chips are also a bug fix: deal events are currently written as ordinary messages from whoever clicked the button, which is why your own screen shows “Dean Hughes accepted the request” as an orange bubble from you. Marking them as events makes them render correctly for both sides.

Confirm three things and I’ll build Round one: (1) split view on desktop, phone unchanged, good? (2) keep the deal panel pinned with its milestone rail, or leave the rail on the order page only? and (3) anything in Rounds two or three you want pulled forward, or dropped?
